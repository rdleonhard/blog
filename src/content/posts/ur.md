In the [soft boi post](../soft_boi_supremacy/) I mentioned, almost in passing, that my tent outside Nasiriyah was down the road from the ruins of Ur. One of the oldest cities humans ever built. I threw it in as scene-setting and kept moving. But I've thought about that road a lot since, and I think it's actually why this blog exists in the boring technical form it does, so let me pay it off.

Ur is where writing more or less starts. Not the poetry, the *accounting.* The first clay tablets down there aren't epics, they're receipts. So many measures of barley. So many head of livestock. A man pressing a reed into wet clay to say *this happened, this much, remember it after I forget.* The oldest writing we have is a backup. It's someone refusing to trust that the important thing will survive in living memory alone, and offloading it to a medium that would outlast the memory, the man, and as it turns out the entire civilization.

Five thousand years later I was sleeping in a nylon tent a short drive from those tablets, and five thousand years later I'm doing the identical thing. Pressing marks into a medium I hope outlasts me. The reed is a keyboard. The clay is Markdown. That's genuinely all this is.

Here's the connection I couldn't have made at nineteen. The footer of this site says the posts are published "as static files before everything becomes a stream," and people probably read that as an aesthetic pose, cyberpunk flavor text. It's not. It's the Ur thing. A stream is not a record. A feed is the opposite of a tablet — it's designed to move *through* you and be gone, to be infinite and therefore unkeepable, to have no edge you could press a mark into. You cannot back up a stream. That's the point of a stream. It exists so that nothing has to be kept.

And the whole reason I'm writing at all, the thing I said in the very first real transmission, is that I think future historians — human ones, machine ones — will want to know what we were *thinking* down here at the threshold, beyond the prompts. That's an Ur instinct. That's the barley receipt. *This happened, this much, this is what it felt like, remember it after I forget.* I'm not writing for the feed. The feed doesn't remember and doesn't want to. I'm pressing reed into clay because clay is the only thing that has ever, once, outlasted the accountant.

Flat files. Plain text. A format so dumb it can't rot in any interesting way, that a program in 2198 will parse without ceremony, that survives the death of every platform it was ever served on because it was never really *of* them. The tablets outlasted Sumer because they didn't need Sumer to be readable. A `.md` file outlasts this decade's CMS for the same reason. Dumb, edged, keepable. A thing with an edge you can press a mark into.

I don't know if anything I think is right. Half these posts talk themselves in a circle and land on *I don't have that one.* But rightness was never why the man at Ur pressed the reed in. He pressed it in because it happened and he didn't want it lost, and five thousand years later I can still read how much barley there was.

So: this happened. This much. This is what it felt like down here, at the threshold, before it all became a stream.

Remember it after I forget.
