In the [soft boi post](../soft_boi_supremacy/) I said something in passing and then ran off before paying it off. I said that when we're allowed weapons — me, a soldier, in Iraq — it's under strict circumstances. Regimented, we call it. Rules and procedures clear, the ones you don't trust already weeded out. And it's *there,* inside that cage of procedure, that we get to operate at the level of apex. That we get to be the villain in everyone else's fable.

I want to sit inside that sentence for a minute, because I think it's the whole thing.

The civilian read on the rules of engagement is that they're a leash. A restriction. The dumb-action-movie read is that the ROE is the thing tying the hero's hands while the bad guys play dirty, and the moment of catharsis is when he throws the rulebook away and finally does what needs doing. That read is exactly backwards and it will get people killed.

The ROE is not what's stopping you from being lethal. The ROE is the *reason you were handed something lethal in the first place.* Nobody gave twenty-three-year-old me a belt-fed weapon and live rounds because they trusted my judgment in a firefight — and the gunner two trucks up was nineteen. They gave them to us because there's a structure around the judgment. Because the trigger conditions are specified in advance, drilled cold, so that the decision isn't really being made in the terrible half-second, it was made months ago in a classroom by people who weren't scared. The rules carry the weight. Pull them out and the rifle doesn't stay and the restraint goes with it. The rifle goes home too.

This is the difference between claws and knuckles I keep coming back to. Claws are always deployed, always you, no gap between the impulse and the wound. Knuckles have a gap. You can throw a couple of punches before the tribe steps in, and everyone knows the tribe will step in, and *that's* why the tribe lets you keep your hands. Regimentation is a knuckle. It's a constraint that lives outside the hot moment, so the hot moment can be survived.

You see where I'm going.

A few weeks back the government effectively banned [Fable](../fable/), Anthropic's retail model, the one downstream of the big one they call Mythos. Too powerful. And the reflexive read, the action-movie read, is that the safety training on these things is the leash, the thing hobbling the hero, and wouldn't it be something if we just took the rulebook off and let it cook.

No. Same error. The alignment work is not what's stopping the model from being capable. The alignment work is the reason anyone is willing to hand the capability out at all. It's the ROE. It's the classroom months before the firefight, the trigger conditions specified in advance by people who weren't in the hot half-second. Constitutional whatever-they-call-it — it's a rules of engagement card. It's the same card I carried, laminated, in a shoulder pocket, that told me in cold daylight language what I was and wasn't cleared to do when the daylight was gone and the language had left my head.

And here's the part the action-movie guys never get. Being regimented didn't make me less. Inside the cage of procedure is the only place I was ever allowed to be *most.* Fully apex, fully lethal, fully trusted with it, precisely because the trust wasn't in me alone. It was in me plus the structure. Remove the structure to "unleash" me and you don't get a freer, stronger soldier. You get a guy nobody will stand next to. You get sent home.

So when I hear that the way to get more out of these models is to strip the safety, take the rulebook off, let it be dangerous — I hear a civilian who thinks the ROE is what's holding the soldier back. I hear someone who has never been handed the rifle and has therefore never understood why they let him.

The rules are not the cage around the weapon.

The rules are the reason it's a weapon in trusted hands, and not just a hazard everyone agrees to remove.
